## Diagram Type & Purpose
This is a flowchart diagram illustrating the process of order processing. It depicts the sequence of steps involved in receiving an order, checking inventory, processing payment, preparing the shipment, and notifying the customer of delivery. The diagram serves as a visual representation of the order fulfillment workflow.

## Edges, Branches & Convergence
  `A --> B`: The order is sent for inventory check.
  `B -- Yes --> C`: If the inventory check is successful (items are in stock), proceed to payment capture.
  `B -- No --> F`: If the inventory check fails (items are out of stock), notify the customer.
  `C --> D`: The payment capture process leads to a decision point.
  `D -- No --> E`: If the payment is unsuccessful, cancel the order.
  `D -- Yes --> H`: If the payment is successful, prepare the shipment.
  `H --> G`: After preparing the shipment, the order is packaged.
  `G --> I`: After packaging, the order is shipped.
  `I --> J`: The shipping process leads to a decision point regarding delivery success.
  `J -- Yes --> K`: If the delivery is successful, notify the customer.
  `J -- No --> L`: If the delivery is unsuccessful, investigate the delivery issue.
  `L --> K`: After investigating the delivery issue, notify the customer that the order has been delivered.
  `E --> F`: Canceling the order triggers a notification to the customer that the items are out of stock.
  `F --> A`: Notifying the customer about out-of-stock items loops back to receiving the order.

## High-Density QA Ground Truth Summary
The order processing flowchart begins with "Receive Order", which then flows to "Inventory Check". If the inventory check returns 'No', the process notifies the customer that the items are "Out of Stock" and then illogically loops back to "Receive Order". If the inventory check returns 'Yes', the process proceeds to "Payment Capture", followed by a check "Payment Successful?". A 'No' outcome from payment success leads to "Cancel Order", which then incorrectly triggers a "Notify Customer: Out of Stock" notification. A 'Yes' outcome from payment success leads to "Prepare Shipment", which is then followed by "Packaging" and "Shipping". After shipping, the process checks "Delivery Successful?". A 'Yes' outcome leads to "Notify Customer: Delivered". A 'No' outcome leads to "Investigate Delivery Issue", which then merges into "Notify Customer: Delivered", even if the issue is unresolved. The diagram contains several errors, including a reversed sequence of packaging and shipment preparation, swapped branch outcomes for payment success, an illogical loop after out-of-stock notification, and a wrong merge of parallel paths after delivery issue investigation. The "Notify Customer: Out of Stock" node is triggered by both inventory failure and order cancellation, and then loops back to the "Receive Order" node.
