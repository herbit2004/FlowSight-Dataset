## Diagram Type & Purpose
This is a flowchart diagram depicting the lifecycle of a ticket in a support or issue tracking system. It illustrates the flow of a ticket from creation to closure, including SLA (Service Level Agreement) checks, escalations, and resolution attempts. The diagram serves as a visual representation of the ticket handling process, aiding in understanding and optimizing the workflow.

## Edges, Branches & Convergence
- `A --> B`: The creation of a ticket triggers the start of the SLA clock.
- `B --> C`: The SLA clock start leads to an initial SLA breach check.
- `C -- SLA Met --> D`: If the SLA is met during the initial check, the ticket is assigned to a support group.
- `C -- SLA Breached --> I`: If the SLA is breached during the initial check, the ticket is escalated to Priority 1.
- `D --> E`: Assignment to a support group leads to an attempt to resolve the ticket.
- `E --> F`: After a resolution attempt, an ongoing SLA breach check is performed.
- `F -- SLA Met --> G`: If the SLA is met during the ongoing check, the ticket is resolved.

## Repository Context & Terminology
- **Ticket**: A record of an issue or request that needs to be addressed.
- **SLA (Service Level Agreement)**: A contract defining the level of service expected from a service provider.
- **SLA Clock**: A timer that tracks the time taken to resolve a ticket, used to measure compliance with the SLA.
- **Escalation**: The process of raising a ticket to a higher priority level or support tier.
- **Support Group**: A team or department responsible for resolving tickets.
- **On-Call Engineer**: An engineer available to respond to urgent issues outside of normal working hours.
- **Ticket Resolution**: The process of addressing and resolving the issue reported in the ticket.
- **Ticket Closure**: The final step in the ticket lifecycle, indicating that the issue has been resolved and the ticket is no longer active.
