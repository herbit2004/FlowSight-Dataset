## Diagram Type & Purpose

It starts with the 'Order Received' node at the top and flows downwards through a series of processes and decision points, ultimately converging at the 'Archive Order' node. ## High-Density QA Ground Truth Summary
The flowchart begins with 'Order Received', immediately followed by a 'Payment Successful?' check. If payment fails ('No' branch), the order is canceled and then archived. If payment succeeds ('Yes' branch), the process moves to 'Prepare Shipment', then checks 'Inventory Available?'. A 'No' on inventory triggers customer notification of delay, then loops back to 'Prepare Shipment'. A 'Yes' on inventory leads to 'Generate Shipping Label', then 'Package Order', then 'Order Shipped', and finally 'Confirm Delivery'. After delivery confirmation, 'Customer Satisfied?' is evaluated. Illogically, a 'Yes' (customer *is* satisfied) leads to 'Archive Order', while a 'No' (customer *not* satisfied) triggers 'Process Refund', which then leads to 'Archive Order'. The 'Archive Order' node then loops back to the 'Order Received' node, restarting the entire process. The standard sequence of packaging before labeling is reversed, and the refund process lacks a completion feedback loop. The illogical loop from 'Archive Order' to 'Order Received' means the process never truly ends.
