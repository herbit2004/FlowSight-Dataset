If the credit card is valid ("Yes" branch), the process moves to "Inventory Check"; otherwise ("No" branch), the customer is notified of payment failure, and the process loops back to "Order Received." After the inventory check, the "Inventory Available?" decision determines whether to "Pack Order" ("Yes" branch) or "Backorder Item" ("No" branch). The "Pack Order" step leads to "Ship Order," which then updates the inventory *before* the backorder process. The "Update Inventory" step feeds back into "Backorder Item," creating an illogical loop. If delivery *is* confirmed ("Yes" branch), the process goes directly to "Close Order," skipping the shipment notification. The "Backorder Item" process also triggers "Send Shipment Notification," which then leads to "Close Order." The final node is "Close Order," marking the completion of the workflow. The diagram contains several errors, including updating inventory before backordering, an illogical loop between "Update Inventory" and "Backorder Item," and a counterintuitive flow where a failed delivery confirmation triggers a shipment notification.
