## Edges, Branches & Convergence
- `A --> B`: The client request is sent to the order placement service.
- `B -- Valid Order --> C`: If the order is valid, the inventory service reserves the stock.
- `C -- Stock Reserved --> D`: If the stock is reserved, the payment service authorizes the payment.
- `D -- Payment Authorized --> E`: If the payment is authorized, the delivery service schedules the delivery.
- `E -- Delivery Scheduled --> F`: If the delivery is scheduled, the order service marks the order as processing.
- `F --> G`: The order service passes control to the success check.
- `G -- Failure --> H`: If the success check fails, the compensation flow is triggered.
- `G -- Success --> M`: If the success check is successful, the order service marks the order as completed.
- `H --> I`: The order service marks the order as failed, triggering the delivery service to cancel the delivery.
- `I --> J`: The delivery service cancels the delivery, triggering the payment service to refund the payment.
- `J --> K`: The payment service refunds the payment, triggering the inventory service to return the stock.
- `K --> L`: The inventory service returns the stock, triggering the notification to the client of failure.
- `M --> N`: The order service marks the order as completed, triggering the notification to the client of success.

## Repository Context & Terminology
- **Transaction Saga**: A sequence of local transactions that together form a single, atomic operation. If one transaction fails, the saga compensates by undoing the work of the previous transactions.
- **Order Placement Service**: A service responsible for handling the initial steps of order creation and validation.
- **Inventory Service**: A service responsible for managing the inventory of products.
- **Payment Service**: A service responsible for handling payment processing.
- **Delivery Service**: A service responsible for scheduling and managing deliveries.
- **Compensation Flow**: A sequence of operations that undo the effects of a failed transaction.

## High-Density QA Ground Truth Summary
The transaction saga begins with a "Client Request" which triggers the "Order Placement Service." If the order is deemed valid, the flow proceeds to the "Inventory Service" to reserve stock. Upon successful stock reservation, the "Payment Service" authorizes the payment. Subsequently, the "Delivery Service" schedules the delivery, and the "Order Service" marks the order as processing. A "Success Check" then determines whether the transaction has been successful. In case of "Failure," a compensation flow is initiated, where the "Order Service" marks the order as failed, the "Delivery Service" cancels the delivery, the "Payment Service" refunds the payment, and the "Inventory Service" returns the stock, ultimately notifying the client of failure. Conversely, upon "Success," the "Order Service" marks the order as completed, and the client is notified of success. The transaction saga pattern ensures data consistency across multiple services by implementing compensating transactions to revert changes made by previous operations in case of failure, maintaining the overall atomicity of the business process.
