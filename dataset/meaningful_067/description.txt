It illustrates the sequence of steps from receiving an order to shipping it, including validation, inventory management, payment processing, and warehouse operations. The diagram serves as a visual representation of the order fulfillment process.

## Edges, Branches & Convergence
- `A --> B`: Represents the flow from receiving the order to validating the order data.
- `B -- Valid --> C`: Represents the flow to inventory check if the order data is valid.
- `B -- Invalid --> D`: Represents the flow to reject the order if the order data is invalid.
- `C -- In Stock --> E`: Represents the flow to allocate inventory if the items are in stock.
- `C -- Out of Stock --> F`: Represents the flow to create a backorder if the items are out of stock.
- `E --> G`: Represents the flow from allocating inventory to payment capture.

- `G -- Payment Successful --> H`: Represents the flow to send a packing request if the payment is successful.

- `J --> K`: Represents the flow from the warehouse picking the items to the order being packed.
- `K --> L`: Represents the flow from the order being packed to scheduling the shipment.
- `L --> M`: Represents the flow from scheduling the shipment to the order being shipped.

## Repository Context & Terminology
- **Order**: A request from a customer to purchase goods or services.
- **Inventory**: The stock of goods held by a company for sale.
- **Backorder**: An order that cannot be filled immediately because the item is out of stock.
- **Payment Capture**: The process of collecting funds from the customer's payment method.
- **Packing Request**: A request sent to the warehouse to pack the items for an order.
- **Shipment Scheduling**: The process of arranging for the transportation of the order to the customer.
- **Order Shipped**: The final stage where the order has left the warehouse and is in transit to the customer.

The warehouse process continues with "Warehouse Picks Items," followed by "Order Packed," and then "Shipment Scheduled." Finally, the process concludes with "Order Shipped," marking the completion of the order fulfillment. The "Inventory Check" node is crucial for determining whether a backorder needs to be created, directly impacting customer order fulfillment timelines.
