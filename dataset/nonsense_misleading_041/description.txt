## High-Density QA Ground Truth Summary
The flowchart begins with a "User Submits Order," which then proceeds to an "Inventory Available?" check. Critically, the 'Yes' and 'No' branches are swapped: if inventory is deemed available (Yes, but labeled No), the system proceeds to "Process Payment"; conversely, if inventory is unavailable (No, but labeled Yes), a "Send 'Out of Stock' Notification" is triggered, followed by "Log Order Failure," and then the process ends at "Order Completed." Assuming inventory is available, the system processes payment and then checks "Payment Successful?". Again, the branches are swapped: a successful payment (Yes, but labeled No) leads to "Ship Order," then "Update Inventory," and finally "Send Shipping Confirmation" before reaching "Order Completed." A failed payment (No, but labeled Yes) triggers "Refund Payment," followed by "Log Payment Failure," and then a check for "Order Failed?". If the order is deemed to have failed (Yes, but labeled No), a "Send 'Payment Failed' Notification" is sent before reaching "Order Completed." However, if the order is not deemed to have failed (No, but labeled Yes), the process loops back to the beginning, "User Submits Order," creating an illogical loop. The inventory update occurs before the shipping confirmation, a reversed sequence from standard practice. The "Order Completed" node serves as the convergence point for all successful and failed paths.
