## Diagram Type & Purpose
This is a flowchart diagram. It depicts the workflow of an order processing system, from receiving an order to archiving its data. The diagram outlines the steps involved in checking payment, inventory, printing shipping labels, and notifying the customer.

## Overall Layout
The diagram has a top-down (TD) layout. It starts with "Order Received" at the top and flows downwards through a series of processes and decision points, ending with "Archive Order Data" at the bottom.

## Subgraphs / Stages
No explicit subgraphs; logically divided into 6 stages: Order Reception & Payment, Inventory Check, Packaging & Shipping Label, Order Shipment, Inventory Update & Notification, and Archiving.

## Edges, Branches & Convergence
- `A --> B`: The order flows from the "Order Received" state to the "Payment Received?" decision.
- `B -- No --> C`: If payment is not received, the flow goes to "Send Order Confirmation".
- `B -- Yes --> D`: If payment is received, the flow goes to "Inventory Check".
- `D -- Available --> E`: If the inventory is available, the flow goes to "Package Order".
- `D -- Unavailable --> F`: If the inventory is unavailable, the flow goes to "Notify Customer of Delay".
- `F --> B`: After notifying the customer of the delay, the flow returns to "Payment Received?".
- `E --> G`: After packaging the order, the flow goes to "Shipping Label Printed?".
- `G -- No --> E`: If the shipping label is not printed, the flow returns to "Package Order".
- `G -- Yes --> H`: If the shipping label is printed, the flow goes to "Order Shipped".
- `C --> H`: After sending the order confirmation, the flow goes to "Order Shipped".
- `H --> I`: After the order is shipped, the flow goes to "Update Inventory".
- `I --> J`: After updating the inventory, the flow goes to "Send Shipment Notification".
- `J --> K`: After sending the shipment notification, the flow goes to "Archive Order Data".

## Repository Context & Terminology
- **Order Received**: Typically signifies the initial capture of customer order details. In this diagram, it's the starting point of the workflow.
- **Payment Received?**: A standard check to ensure payment authorization. Here, a 'No' outcome leads to sending an order confirmation, which is unusual.
- **Inventory Check**: A process to verify product availability. In this diagram, unavailability leads to customer notification and a loop back to payment.
- **Package Order**: The physical or logical process of preparing an order for shipment. Here, failure to print a shipping label loops back to re-packaging.
- **Shipping Label Printed?**: Checks if a shipping label has been successfully generated. In this diagram, a negative outcome loops back to packaging.
- **Order Shipped**: Indicates the order has left the warehouse. In this diagram, it follows both successful shipping label printing and sending order confirmation regardless of payment.
- **Update Inventory**: Adjusting stock levels after shipment. In this diagram, this happens *before* sending shipment notification.
- **Send Shipment Notification**: Informing the customer that their order has shipped. In this diagram, it occurs after inventory update.
- **Archive Order Data**: Storing the order details for record-keeping. In this diagram, it's the final step.

## High-Density QA Ground Truth Summary

If payment is not received (B -- No --> C), an "Order Confirmation" is sent, and the process jumps to "Order Shipped" (H). If payment *is* received (B -- Yes --> D), an "Inventory Check" (D) occurs. If the inventory is "Unavailable" (D -- Unavailable --> F), the customer is notified of a delay (F), and the process loops back to "Payment Received?" (B). If the inventory is "Available" (D -- Available --> E), the order is packaged (E), and the system checks if the "Shipping Label Printed?" (G). If not (G -- No --> E), the system loops back to "Package Order" (E). If the shipping label *is* printed (G -- Yes --> H), the order is marked as "Order Shipped" (H). From "Order Shipped" (H), the system "Update Inventory" (I), then "Send Shipment Notification" (J), and finally "Archive Order Data" (K). The "Notify Customer of Delay" (F) node incorrectly loops back to the "Payment Received?" (B) node, creating a potential infinite loop. The "Update Inventory" (I) step occurs *before* "Send Shipment Notification" (J), which is counter-intuitive.
