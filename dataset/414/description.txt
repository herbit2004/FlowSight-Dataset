## Diagram Type & Purpose
This is an architecture diagram depicting a three-layer quantum computing system. It illustrates the flow from high-level problem builders to quantum solvers and finally to quantum backends. The diagram serves as a high-level overview of the system's architecture, showing the relationships between different components.

## Overall Layout
The diagram is laid out in a top-down (TD) manner, with three distinct layers arranged vertically. The flow starts at the top layer with high-level builders and progresses downwards through quantum solvers to quantum backends. There are multiple start nodes in the top layer and multiple end nodes in the bottom layer.

## Subgraphs / Stages
The diagram contains three explicit subgraphs, representing the three layers of the quantum computing system:
- **Layer 1: High-Level Builders**: Contains components responsible for creating problem instances.
- **Layer 2: Quantum Solvers**: Contains quantum algorithms (QAOA) to solve the problems.
- **Layer 3: Quantum Backends**: Contains different quantum computing hardware platforms.

Each builder creates a problem instance, which is then passed to a corresponding quantum solver in the second layer. The solvers then send the problem to various quantum backends in the third layer for execution. These backends include LocalBackend (≤20 qubits), IonQBackend (Azure Quantum), RigettiBackend (Azure Quantum), AtomComputingBackend (Azure Quantum, 100+ qubits), and QuantinuumBackend (Azure Quantum, 99.9%+ fidelity). The diagram highlights the modularity of the system, allowing different problem builders to utilize various quantum solvers and backends. The Azure Quantum service provides access to the IonQ, Rigetti, AtomComputing, and Quantinuum backends, enabling users to leverage different quantum hardware platforms.
